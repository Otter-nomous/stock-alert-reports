Market SummaryRead ▾

Broad-based selloff hit major indices overnight—SPY down 1.70%, QQQ down 1.85%, with leveraged tech ETFs TQQQ and SOXL cratering 5.71% and 6.76% respectively. The damage concentrated in semiconductors and related hardware: SMCI imploded 33.3% after co-founder Wally Liaw's indictment for alleged Nvidia chip smuggling to China; downstream suppliers WDC (-7.5%) and STX (-5.4%) caught shrapnel from the same investigation. Beyond chips, utilities took fire (CEG -10.9% on JPMorgan downgrade, VST -12.8% ahead of ex-dividend), MOS cratered 10% on BofA's phosphate margin concerns, and geopolitical jitters over Middle East tensions spilled into industrials and materials. SNDK's 8.1% drop signals profit-taking after last week's 25% surge.

Recommendation

Avoid semiconductor exposure near-term—the SMCI investigation creates headline risk across the entire chip ecosystem, and WDC, STX, and INTC remain vulnerable to spillover selling. Utilities warrant caution given valuation pressure (CEG) and ex-dividend volatility (VST). However, IRM's 5.1% decline after a 29% three-month run looks like a genuine pullback in a fundamentally sound data center play—consider accumulating on weakness. For broad exposure, patient capital should wait for stabilization signals before re-entering tech; current levels may not yet reflect capitulation.
