Pre-Close · March 20, 2026
Broad equity weakness rippled through markets overnight, with the Nasdaq-100 (QQQ) falling 1.85% and leveraged semiconductor plays (SOXL) plummeting 6.76% after federal prosecutors indicted Super Micro Computer's co-founder for allegedly smuggling $2.5 billion in restricted AI servers to China. The indictment triggered a domino effect across chip stocks and export-sensitive sectors, while energy and utilities also retreated amid geopolitical tensions and profit-taking in recent outperformers. SMCI itself cratered 33.3%, but contagion spread to peers like Intel (-5.0%), Western Digital (-7.5%), and Seagate (-5.4%), signaling investor concern over potential export restrictions and supply chain risks.
Sectors  Technology absorbed the heaviest losses as semiconductor and networking equipment makers fell on export restriction fears and competitive share losses in AI infrastructure. Utilities and energy suffered alongside, with Vistra (-12.8%) and NRG (-9.7%) retreating after recent rallies, while Middle East tensions and margin pressures hammered basic materials (Mosaic -10.0%) and industrial names (Comfort Systems -6.1%). Healthcare, Real Estate, and Industrials also posted declines, though the day's story remained firmly centered on tech sector vulnerability to geopolitical and regulatory headwinds.
