Pre-Close · March 20, 2026
Broad-based selling gripped markets overnight, with QQQ and leveraged tech ETFs (TQQQ, SOXL) posting losses of 1.85%–6.76% as semiconductor and utility stocks bore the brunt of the selloff. The standout catalyst was Super Micro Computer's 33% crash following co-founder charges related to chip smuggling to China—a rare compliance shock that rattled confidence in the sector. Profit-taking in recent winners (Vistra, SanDisk) combined with fundamental concerns over competition (Western Digital vs. Seagate), margin pressures (Mosaic), and valuation reassessment across tech created a perfect storm for growth-heavy names.
Market Summary

Technology and utilities led declines, with semiconductors (WDC -7.5%, STX -5.4%, INTC -5.0%) and storage names (SMCI -33.3%) hit hardest by competitive and legal headwinds, while utilities (VST -12.8%, NRG -9.7%, CEG -10.9%) reversed recent gains amid profit-taking and investor reassessment. Basic materials (MOS -10.0%) and industrials (FIX -6.1%) also declined on sector-specific headwinds—BofA downgrade and broad construction weakness, respectively—signaling that weakness extended well beyond growth stocks into economically-sensitive names.
